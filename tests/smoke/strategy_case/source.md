# Northwind Tea House — strategic analysis (case outline)

Northwind runs a chain of tea bars for young urban customers aged 18-30.

## Firm analysis
### Social space
- Customer need: young customers want a cozy place to meet friends at night; 62% name social needs as the main reason to visit.
- What Northwind does: a relaxed tea-bar format with no table charge and music events.
- Performance: customer satisfaction is 93%; the repeat-visit rate is 88%.
- Implication: the service attracts the target customers, but the format is easy to copy.

### Value for money
- Customer need: 58% of young customers say price matters when they choose a place.
- What Northwind does: own-brand drinks at RMB 15-25, below most rivals.
- Performance: revenue grew from RMB 210M in 2021 to RMB 540M in 2023.
- Implication: customers are price sensitive, so raising prices is hard.

## Store growth
- Store count: 120 stores in 2021, 260 in 2022 and 410 in 2023.
- Store-level net margin is 24%, against 16% for the peer average.
- Implication: cost control funds fast expansion, but it is not a lasting barrier.

## Competition (five forces, short)
- New entrants: low capital needs; rent and fit-out are the main costs. Influence on Northwind: the entry barrier is low; expect more copycats.
- Rivals: the top 5 chains hold 6% of the market; two rivals copy the format in tier-2 cities. Influence on Northwind: price competition in tier-1 and tier-2 cities.
- Substitutes: KTV, board-game cafes and restaurants serve the same night-time social need; the switching cost is zero. Influence on Northwind: loyalty must come from the experience, not from price.

## Strategic challenge
- Driver: the format is easy to copy. Possible result: rivals erode the differentiation.
- Driver: customers have low loyalty. Possible result: visits move to whatever is new.
- Driver: revenue comes from store operation only. Possible result: growth is tied to opening stores.
- Key threat: revenue growth per store slows as rivals open nearby.
- Key question: How can Northwind keep revenue growth sustainable, given rivals copying its format?

## Options
### Option A — Retail channels
- What: sell own-brand bottled tea drinks through e-commerce and convenience stores.
- How it addresses the challenge: a second growth engine that does not depend on opening stores; the brand reaches customers outside the bar.
- Key activities: product — adapt two existing drinks for bottling; sales — build a channel team for e-commerce and convenience stores.
- New value proposition: Northwind drinks wherever young customers shop.
- Advantages: second growth engine; scale lowers unit cost.
- Disadvantages: competes with beverage leaders; may reduce store visits.

### Option B — Premium sub-brand
- What: open a quieter, premium tea-bar format in tier-1 business districts.
- Positioning: Northwind today sits in the mid-price tier; the sub-brand would move into the premium tier.
- How it addresses the challenge: a new segment with less direct rivalry.
- Key activities: product — premium drinks menu; stores — pilot stores in business districts.
- New value proposition: a quiet, premium place to meet at night.
- Advantages: higher spend per visit; less direct rivalry.
- Disadvantages: the new brand starts with low awareness; higher fit-out cost.

### Option C — Partner network
- What: a membership alliance with board-game cafes and cinemas.
- How it addresses the challenge: more reasons to visit through shared membership.
- Key activities: partners — sign board-game cafes and cinemas; membership — one shared card and joint offers.
- New value proposition: one membership for a night out.
- Advantages: low investment; shared marketing.
- Disadvantages: weak control over partners; benefits are mostly short-term.

## Scorecard (1 = poor, 5 = excellent)
| Criterion | Weight | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market size | 30% | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Fit with capabilities | 25% | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Competitive intensity | 25% | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| Risk | 20% | 4 | 3 | 3 |

## Recommendation
- Recommend Option A first and Option B second.
- Short term: launch two bottled drinks on e-commerce in 2025, then convenience stores in tier-1 cities.
- Long term: pilot the premium sub-brand with 5 stores in 2026.
