# Meridian Bank: Winning Small-Business Banking With a Digital Platform

Audience: Meridian Bank board strategy committee. Purpose: choose a digital strategy for small and medium enterprise (SME) banking.

Slide 1: Title Slide
Title: Winning SME Banking Digitally
Subtitle: Options and recommendation for the board strategy committee

Slide 2: Executive Summary
- Situation: SMEs generate 31% of Meridian's revenue but our share of new SME accounts fell from 18% to 12% in three years.
- Complication: digital-only lenders approve SME loans in 24 hours; our process takes 11 days.
- Key question: how should Meridian compete for SMEs over the next three years?
- Recommendation: build an SME platform with partners (Option B), launching in 2026.

Slide 3: Lending Is Half of the SME Revenue Pool
SME banking revenue pool in our markets, 2025 (USD million): working-capital loans 840, term loans 610, payments and cards 420, deposits 380, foreign exchange 190, insurance 110.

Slide 4: The Most Valuable SMEs Sit Where Three Needs Overlap
Customer research found three overlapping needs: digital-first SMEs (run the business on their phone), credit-hungry SMEs (need fast working capital) and cash-heavy SMEs (still collect cash). SMEs that are both digital-first and credit-hungry are the most profitable group; those in all three groups are the hardest to serve.

Slide 5: Digital Lenders Lead on Digital Maturity, We Lead on SME Focus
Six competitors rated 1–10 on digital maturity and on SME focus:
- Meridian: digital maturity 4, SME focus 8
- KasiPay: digital maturity 9, SME focus 7
- FinNova: digital maturity 8, SME focus 4
- Coastline Bank: digital maturity 5, SME focus 5
- Pacific Trust: digital maturity 3, SME focus 3
- LendLoop: digital maturity 9, SME focus 9

Slide 6: Three Options for the Board
- Option A, Digitise in-house: rebuild our SME journey ourselves; investment USD 60M; launch in 30 months; pros: full control, uses existing data; cons: slow, scarce engineering talent.
- Option B, Platform with partners: open banking platform with accounting, payroll and e-commerce partners; investment USD 35M; launch in 14 months; pros: fast, partners bring customers; cons: shared economics, partner dependency.
- Option C, Acquire a digital lender: buy LendLoop; investment USD 220M; launch in 6 months; pros: immediate capability; cons: price, integration risk, culture.

Slide 7: Option B Has the Most Balanced Capability Profile
Each option rated 1–5 on six criteria:

| Criterion | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | 1 | 4 | 5 |
| Cost | 3 | 5 | 1 |
| Control | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Customer reach | 2 | 5 | 3 |
| Risk | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| Data advantage | 4 | 4 | 3 |

Slide 8: The Platform Becomes a Self-Reinforcing Growth Engine
More SMEs on the platform produce more transaction data; more data improves credit decisions; better credit decisions allow lower loan pricing; lower pricing attracts more SMEs to the platform.

Slide 9: The Operating Model Is Built in Four Layers
From top to bottom:
- Customer channels: mobile app, relationship managers, partner apps
- SME products: loans, payments, FX, insurance
- Data and decision platform: credit scoring, fraud, pricing
- Core banking and open APIs

Slide 10: Five Partner Types Plug Into the Platform
At the centre is the Meridian SME platform; around it: accounting software providers, payroll providers, e-commerce marketplaces, logistics firms and insurance carriers.

Slide 11: Implementation in Three Phases to 2028
- 2026 H1 — Foundation: open APIs, two accounting partners, credit model v1
- 2026 H2–2027 — Launch: mobile app, 24-hour loan decision, payroll and e-commerce partners
- 2028 — Scale: insurance and logistics partners, regional roll-out
